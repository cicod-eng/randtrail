Binance vs OKX in South Africa (2026)

Last updated: 12 September 2026

For most South Africans the deciding factor between Binance and OKX is how rand gets in and out — not a fraction of a percent on trading fees. Binance offers more direct ZAR routes (linked-bank deposit, card, Apple Pay), while OKX leans on P2P and card for rand. Neither holds an FSCA CASP licence.

Fees and routes change, so treat the figures below as a starting point and confirm the current numbers in each app before acting.

Quick verdict

  • Smoothest rand on-ramp — Binance, with linked-bank ZAR deposits plus card and Apple Pay on top of P2P.
  • Lowest spot fee — close: OKX around 0.08% maker / 0.10% taker at the regular tier; Binance commonly 0.10% with a BNB discount.
  • FSCA licence — neither. Both are global platforms; a licensed exchange (VALR, Luno) is the alternative if that is your priority.
  • Choose OKX for trading tools and Web3; choose Binance for the deepest market and easiest rand funding.

ZAR access: where they really differ

This is the difference most South African users feel first. Binance supports direct rand funding through a linked South African bank account, card, Apple Pay and P2P (Google Pay is not currently supported for ZAR), and it has run ZAR deposit fee campaigns at times. OKX’s rand on-ramp is built mainly around P2P (bank transfer, options like FNB eWallet) and card — card purchases may include a processing fee, so check the fee shown before you confirm.

If you want the simplest rand deposit, Binance is usually the smoother option on paper, but confirm the routes shown for your own account. If you are happy buying through P2P, both Binance and OKX work. For a full walkthrough of moving rand into USDT, see How to buy USDT with ZAR.

Fees: closer than the headlines suggest

At the regular tier, spot fees are essentially in line — Binance is commonly cited at 0.10% (discounted when paid in BNB), while OKX is around 0.08% maker and 0.10% taker (discounted with OKB). Both offer free crypto deposits and network-dependent withdrawal fees. The bigger cost driver for South Africans is usually how you move rand: a card surcharge on OKX or a conversion spread can outweigh a 0.02% trading-fee difference many times over.

Regulatory status: the same caveat

Neither Binance nor OKX holds an FSCA CASP licence. Binance is FIC-registered for AML and offers certain derivatives through an FSCA-authorised partner as juristic representative; OKX operates through its global entity. In practice this means you are using a global platform rather than a locally licensed CASP. If local licensing and a domestic escalation channel matter to you, compare a licensed exchange such as VALR or Luno. See Is Binance legal in South Africa? for the detail.

Two OKX points worth knowing: it offers a crypto card that covers South Africa, and in 2025 it reached a settlement with US authorities over past anti-money-laundering gaps. The settlement does not affect day-to-day trading, but it is part of the full picture when you weigh trust.

Which should you choose?

  • Pick Binance if you want the easiest rand funding (linked-bank ZAR deposit, card or Apple Pay), the deepest market, and a broad stablecoin ecosystem.
  • Pick OKX if you value trading tools, a strong Web3 wallet, and are comfortable funding via P2P or card.
  • Pick a licensed local exchange (VALR, Luno) if an FSCA CASP licence is your deciding factor.
