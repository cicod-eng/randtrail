How to Deposit ZAR on Binance in South Africa

Last updated: 12 September 2026

Binance currently lets eligible South African users fund their account with rand through a few routes — Absa Pay, Capitec Pay, card, Apple Pay and a bank transfer (EFT). There is no single “best” method for everyone: the right one depends on which bank you use, how fast you need the money in, and the fee shown for your own account. Check the fee and the amount you will receive before you confirm.

One clarification up front, because it trips people up: a ZAR deposit puts rand into your Binance fiat balance so you can then buy crypto. P2P is different — there you pay a seller directly and receive the crypto from them, and the rand never sits in your fiat balance. Both are covered below.

Quick answer

  • Absa Pay — a direct ZAR deposit route Binance documents for South African users; choose it in the app under Add Funds → Deposit ZAR.
  • Capitec Pay — added in January 2026 for Capitec clients; you approve the payment in the Capitec app without entering card details.
  • Card or Apple Pay — the fastest route, with no bank transfer to wait for, but it may carry a card processing fee.
  • Bank transfer (EFT) — deposit from a linked South African bank account; instant EFT clears quickly, a standard EFT can take one to two business days.
  • P2P — buy crypto straight from a seller with rand, instead of depositing rand first.
  • Google Pay — currently not supported for ZAR deposits on Binance; use card or Apple Pay instead.
  • Before you confirm — complete identity verification and check the exact fee and receive amount shown for your account.

Methods, fees, limits and availability change and vary by account, bank and payment provider. Check what is shown in your own account before you act.

Method 1 — Absa Pay

Absa Pay is a ZAR deposit route Binance documents specifically for South African users, with an official support FAQ published in July 2026. If you bank with Absa, this is the most direct option to check first.

Step 1: Open Deposit ZAR

In the Binance app, tap Add Funds → Deposit ZAR. On the website, go to Deposit, pick ZAR, then choose Absa Pay.

Step 2: Verify if prompted

If you have not completed identity verification, you will be asked to do it here. Verification affects which ZAR routes and limits you see.

Step 3: Enter the amount and check the fee

Enter how much rand you want to deposit, then review the fee and the amount you will actually receive before continuing.

Step 4: Pay and wait for credit

Tap Pay Now and follow the on-screen prompts. Once the payment is processed and confirmed, the funds are credited to your Binance account. For the full picture of Binance’s ZAR routes, fees and safety in South Africa, see our Binance South Africa review.

Method 2 — Capitec Pay

Capitec Pay was added in January 2026 as a more direct way for Capitec clients to fund Binance. The key detail is that approval happens inside the Capitec app — you do not enter card details or share online-banking login information, which some users find reassuring. Binance’s Capitec Pay FAQ walks through the flow.

Step 1: Select Capitec Pay

In the app tap Add Funds → Deposit ZAR, then choose Capitec Pay as the method. On the website, click Buy Crypto, switch to the Deposit Fiat tab, and select Deposit ZAR → Capitec Pay.

Step 2: Enter the amount

Enter the rand amount and tap Pay Now. Complete identity verification first if you are prompted.

Step 3: Approve in the Capitec app

Approve the payment in your Capitec banking app when prompted. This is the step that authorises the money to leave your account.

Step 4: Wait for credit

Once the payment is confirmed, the ZAR is credited to your Binance account. If a deposit fails, check that the name on your Capitec account matches the name on your Binance account, and that your limits allow the transaction.

Method 3 — Card or Apple Pay

Paying by Visa or Mastercard, or with Apple Pay, is the fastest way to get rand in because there is no bank transfer to wait for. Binance’s card and Apple Pay FAQ confirms both are supported. Note the official wording on Google Pay: it is currently not supported, so use card or Apple Pay instead.

Step 1: Verify your account

Complete identity verification first; it affects which payment methods and limits you see.

Step 2: Choose card or Apple Pay

Select ZAR as the currency and choose the card or Apple Pay route, then enter the amount.

Step 3: Check the fee before you confirm

Card deposits can be instant but may carry a small card processing fee. Review the fee and final amount shown, then confirm.

Method 4 — Bank transfer (EFT)

Binance has supported ZAR deposits from a linked South African bank account since it launched its rand gateway, with the transfer handled by a payment partner. Binance’s own South Africa buying guide lists an instant EFT option (funds arrive instantly) and a standard bank transfer that can take one to two business days. Binance’s current South Africa page also states that there is no minimum ZAR deposit requirement, although payment-provider limits may still apply.

Step 1: Verify and link your bank account

Complete identity verification, then link a bank account in your own name. The supported banks have historically covered South Africa’s main banks, but check the current list shown in your account.

Step 2: Choose instant EFT or standard transfer

Pick the speed you need. Instant EFT credits quickly; a standard transfer is slower but may suit you if the instant option is not available.

Step 3: Confirm the amount and fee

Enter the amount and check any fee and the receive amount shown before you confirm.

Method 5 — P2P (buy crypto straight from a seller)

If your goal is simply to own crypto with your rand, P2P skips the deposit step entirely: you pay a seller in rand and receive the crypto directly. On the Binance P2P ZAR market, sellers list offers against rand and you pick one that suits your bank and amount. Binance is currently running a zero-fee promotion for ZAR P2P pairs until 31 October 2026, so no maker or taker fee is charged during that window. Outside promotions, maker or taker fees may apply, so check the current fee schedule. The seller’s margin can also be reflected in the quoted exchange rate. Pay only from a payment method in your own name, and only confirm after the money has actually left your account. For the full steps and the scams to watch, read our P2P guide for South Africa.

What it really costs

  • Absa Pay and Capitec Pay — the fee is shown to you before you pay. There have been limited-time promotions (for example, a Capitec Pay zero-fee campaign in early 2026, and a ZAR deposit fee-discount period later in the year), but do not assume a promotion is still running — check the fee shown at the time.
  • Card — card purchases may include a processing fee on top of the amount, shown before you confirm.
  • Instant EFT / bank deposit — fees have changed over time and Binance has run temporary deposit-fee promotions. Check the exact fee shown before confirming rather than relying on an older percentage.
  • P2P — during the current ZAR P2P zero-fee promotion (until 31 October 2026) there is no maker or taker fee, but outside promotions fees may apply and the seller’s margin is normally reflected in the quoted ZAR/USDT price.
  • Buying after you deposit — funding the account is only the first step. Your final cost can also include the trading fee, bid-ask spread or the quote used by a quick-buy or convert tool, depending on how you buy.

Which method should you pick?

Match the method to your bank and your urgency rather than chasing a headline number:

  • You bank with Absa → try Absa Pay first; it is the most direct documented route for your bank.
  • You bank with Capitec → Capitec Pay keeps approval inside your Capitec app and avoids typing card details.
  • You need it now and will accept a card fee → card or Apple Pay, the fastest option.
  • You use another SA bank → a linked-account bank transfer or instant EFT.
  • You just want crypto, not a rand balance → P2P, and compare the seller’s rate against the market.

Whichever you choose, the number that matters is how much crypto (or spendable rand) you end up with after every fee — not the advertised method.

Mistakes that cost South Africans money

  • Skipping identity verification, then finding the ZAR deposit route you wanted is not showing for your account.
  • Assuming a fee promotion is still live without checking — several ZAR deposit campaigns in 2026 were limited-time.
  • Trying to use Google Pay and finding it is currently not supported for ZAR deposits.
  • Paying a P2P seller and confirming the order before the transfer has actually cleared your bank account.
  • Forgetting that a deposit is separate from the buy — the trading fee and spread still apply after the money lands.
