Is Binance Legal in South Africa? (2026)

Last updated: 12 September 2026

South African individuals can currently access and use Binance, and we found no current FSCA prohibition on individuals opening an account or trading crypto on the platform. The key distinction is on the licensing side: Binance is accessible to South African users, but it does not appear on the FSCA’s authorised-CASP list as of our September 2026 check. That is a licensing gap, not a ban.

This guide explains what that difference actually means — “accessible to use” versus “holds a local licence” — how Binance is structured for South African users, and what it means for your tax and record-keeping.

Quick answer

  • Can South Africans use Binance? Yes — Binance is currently accessible to South African users.
  • Does Binance hold an FSCA CASP licence? Not according to the FSCA’s published authorised-CASP list at our September 2026 check.
  • Is Binance banned? We found no current FSCA prohibition on South African individuals using Binance.
  • Derivatives? Certain products use a separate FiveWest juristic-representative structure.
  • Tax? Using Binance does not remove your SARS reporting obligations.

What “legal” means for a crypto exchange in South Africa

Crypto assets were declared financial products under the FAIS framework in October 2022, and the Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA) is the regulator. Existing crypto service providers were then given a transitional period to apply for authorisation, with the application deadline falling in November 2023.

The important distinction is between a locally licensed CASP and a global platform. Local exchanges such as VALR and Luno operate through locally authorised entities and appear on the FSCA’s authorised-CASP list. Binance and OKX do not appear on that list as of our latest check — they serve South African users from overseas. Neither situation is “illegal”; they are simply different regulatory structures.

Does Binance hold an FSCA CASP licence?

No. As of our latest check in September 2026, Binance does not appear on the FSCA’s published list of authorised CASPs. We phrase it as a point-in-time check deliberately — licensing status can change, so this is something to re-verify rather than treat as permanent.

Not being on the list does not mean Binance is banned or that using it is against the law. It means Binance does not hold the same local licence a South African exchange holds. See FSCA & CASP licensing explained for the full framework.

How Binance is structured for South African users

For certain derivatives products, South African residents are onboarded through Binance-group entities — including Nest Exchange Limited and Binance Bahrain B.S.C. (c) — which act as juristic representatives of FiveWest OTC Desk (Pty) Ltd, an authorised South African financial services provider.

That is a specific arrangement for those products, and it should not be read as Binance itself holding an FSCA CASP licence. The two things are easy to conflate but they are different.

Binance also applies South Africa’s Travel Rule requirements. From 30 April 2025, South African users began being asked for additional sender or beneficiary information when depositing or withdrawing crypto.

Is it illegal to use Binance as an individual?

No. There is no South African law that makes it illegal for an individual to open a Binance account, deposit rand or trade crypto. The responsibilities that sit on you are the normal ones: complete identity verification honestly, comply with any KYC or FICA checks, keep records, and declare any tax obligations to SARS.

One thing worth understanding is that a global platform’s licensing and escalation path sit outside South Africa. If you prefer a local licence and a domestic channel for complaints, that is a reason to compare a licensed exchange such as VALR or Luno — not because Binance is illegal, but because the structures differ.

Tax: Binance does not change your SARS position

Using Binance instead of a local exchange does not change how SARS treats your crypto. SARS treats crypto assets under normal tax principles — gains may be revenue in nature or capital in nature depending on the facts. Keep records of purchases, sales, transfers and fees — you may need them to complete your return. See Crypto tax in South Africa for the detail.

Frequently asked questions

Is Binance banned in South Africa?

No. Binance is not banned in South Africa. It does not appear on the FSCA’s authorised-CASP list as of our September 2026 check, but that is a licensing status, not a prohibition on individual users.

Does Binance have an FSCA licence?

No. Binance does not appear on the FSCA’s published authorised-CASP list as of our latest check. It uses a separate arrangement for certain derivatives products through FiveWest OTC Desk (Pty) Ltd, but that is not the same as Binance itself holding a CASP licence.

Can SARS see my Binance account?

Do not assume crypto activity on an overseas exchange is invisible to SARS. South Africa’s Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) took effect on 1 March 2026 and requires relevant crypto service providers to collect and report certain user and transaction information. SARS also states that it may receive crypto data from both local and international service providers.

This does not mean every Binance account is automatically visible to SARS in real time, but users should keep accurate records and declare taxable crypto activity correctly.

Is it legal to buy USDT on Binance in South Africa?

Yes. Buying, holding or trading USDT on Binance is legal for individuals in South Africa. You may have tax obligations on any gains, but the activity itself is not illegal.

Should I use Binance or a licensed South African exchange?

That depends on what matters to you. Choose Binance if you want deep markets, wide stablecoin support and multiple ZAR funding routes. Choose a licensed exchange such as VALR or Luno if a local FSCA CASP licence is your priority. If you are comparing global platforms, see Binance vs OKX. If local licensing is your priority, compare Binance with VALR or Luno.
