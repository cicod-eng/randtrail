How to Cash Out Crypto as Cash at an ATM in South Africa

Last updated: 15 September 2026

South Africa now has a way to turn crypto into rand cash without a bank account: CryptoExpress, a free app from Paycorp, lets you send Bitcoin, Ethereum, USDC or USDT from your own wallet and collect rand from a Cash Express ATM using a voucher PIN. It is genuinely useful for small, cash-in-hand withdrawals — but it is not built for large amounts, and the cost is not as transparent as a bank withdrawal. This guide explains what it actually is, what it really costs and when you should use a licensed exchange instead.

One thing to settle early: the ATM is only one off-ramp among several. For the full menu of ways to get rand out of crypto — bank withdrawal, local exchange or P2P — start with our Cash Out overview. This page zooms in on the cash-at-an-ATM route.

Quick answer

  • What it is — a cardless cash-out app from Paycorp (ATMs run by its subsidiary ATM Solutions), with crypto-to-fiat conversion handled by payment processor Triple-A. You send crypto from your own wallet, get a voucher PIN, and collect rand.
  • Which coins — Bitcoin, Ethereum, USDC and USDT, paid out in rand. Rand is the only cash currency.
  • Per-withdrawal cap — reported at R5,000 in third-party coverage (described as an ATM hardware limit, not a regulation). Paycorp does not publish an official limit, so confirm in the app.
  • The cost — an undisclosed “facilitation fee” is built into the quoted exchange rate. Compare the quote with the live market rate before you accept it.
  • KYC — light sign-up to start, plus ID verification once your cumulative withdrawals pass a threshold reported around R25,000.
  • Tax — converting crypto to cash is a disposal, so it can be a taxable event under SARS rules.

What CryptoExpress actually is

CryptoExpress is not a “bitcoin ATM” in the classic sense. There is no machine that scans a QR code and counts out rand based on the live BTC price. It is a cardless cash-out system: you authorise a transaction in your own wallet, the app issues a voucher PIN, and you collect rand at a Cash Express ATM. You do not need a bank card at any point.

The operator is Paycorp, a payments company headquartered in South Africa, and the ATMs are run by its subsidiary ATM Solutions. Paycorp announced the service in November 2023 and publicised it in July 2024, describing a network of “more than 3,000” crypto-enabled Cash Express ATMs nationwide. The crypto-to-fiat conversion, KYC and anti-money-laundering screening are handled by Triple-A, a payment processor Paycorp partnered with for the service.

The official site is explicit that CryptoExpress is not a trading platform. You are not placing orders on a book; you are converting a set amount at a quoted rate, purely to withdraw it as cash. That matters, because the “rate” you accept is where the cost hides — more on that below.

Which coins and networks it supports

The app handles cash-outs for four assets, all paid out in rand:

  • Bitcoin (BTC)
  • Ethereum (ETH)
  • USD Coin (USDC)
  • Tether (USDT)

Rand is the only cash currency available — you cannot collect dollars or another fiat currency at the ATM.

For the two stablecoins, third-party guides report that deposits go through the Tron (TRC-20) or Ethereum (ERC-20) networks. The rule that matters most: send on exactly the network the app shows you for that withdrawal. Sending USDT on the wrong chain is one of the most common ways funds get lost, and a wrong-network transfer may not be recoverable. If you are unsure how networks, addresses and memos work, read our Transfers & networks guide before your first withdrawal.

How it works, step by step

Step 1: Download the app and complete light KYC

Install CryptoExpress for iOS or Android and register. The sign-up asks for basic details — full name, email address, mobile number, date of birth and gender — which are screened against sanctions lists. This is the light, Level 1 verification; the threshold for deeper checks is covered under limits below.

Step 2: Start a withdrawal and accept the quoted rate

Choose the cryptocurrency and the rand amount you want. The app shows a conversion quote, and you must accept it to continue. This is the moment to compare the quote with the current market rate — the difference is your all-in cost.

Step 3: Send the exact amount on the correct network

The app gives you a deposit address. Send the exact amount from your own wallet on the network the app specifies. On-chain confirmation can take up to about 30 minutes before the voucher is issued, so this is not an instant, tap-and-go process.

Step 4: Receive your voucher PIN

Once the transaction confirms, the app generates a 10-digit withdrawal voucher code. Keep it private — anyone with your mobile number and PIN can collect the cash.

Step 5: Collect rand at a Cash Express ATM

Use the app’s ATM locator to find a crypto-enabled Cash Express machine, select the CryptoExpress option, enter your mobile number and PIN, and take the cash.

What it really costs

Here is the part most coverage skips: Paycorp does not publish a fee schedule. It quotes what it calls a “fair exchange rate” that includes an undisclosed facilitation fee, and independent reviewers describe the fee as variable and not transparent. In other words, the cost sits inside the exchange rate you are offered, not in a separate line item.

The way to see it for yourself: note the rand amount the app offers, then compare it with the current market rate for the same amount from a public source — a stablecoin’s USD/ZAR rate, or BTC/ZAR on a South African exchange. The gap between the two is roughly your all-in cost (the fee plus the spread). Do that comparison at least once before you commit, so you know what the convenience is actually costing you.

The trade-off, to be fair: CryptoExpress uses real-time on-chain hedging, which locks the quoted rate for the duration of the transaction so you are not exposed to price swings while the transfer confirms. That certainty is part of what the built-in fee pays for. Whether it is worth it depends on the amount and on what a bank or exchange would charge you — which is exactly the judgement you should make case by case.

Limits and verification

The figure repeated most often is a R5,000 cap per withdrawal, described in third-party coverage as an ATM cash-dispensing (hardware) limit rather than a regulatory one. Paycorp does not publish official limits, so treat R5,000 as a practical ceiling and check what your own app shows. If you need more than that, the ATM route means repeat visits — at which point a bank withdrawal on a licensed exchange is usually simpler and cheaper.

On verification: the starting KYC is light. Third-party reporting says that once your cumulative withdrawals pass around R25,000, you must complete a deeper verification by uploading a valid ID document such as a passport or driver’s licence. The threshold and exact documents may change, so follow what the app asks of you.

When the ATM is the wrong choice

CryptoExpress fills a specific gap — small amounts, turned into cash, without a bank account or card. Step outside that gap and the ATM stops making sense:

  • Amounts above R5,000 — you would need multiple withdrawals, each with its own embedded fee. A licensed exchange bank transfer is the cleaner route for larger sums.
  • You want the best rate — the hidden facilitation fee usually makes a bank withdrawal cheaper once the amount is meaningful. Compare before you choose.
  • You need a clean paper trail — a bank transfer leaves a clearer record for loans, proof of funds or your SARS declaration. Cash is harder to document.

For those situations, work through the exchange route instead: see our Cash Out overview, the Sell Crypto guide and the step-by-step How to withdraw from Binance walkthrough.

Tax — cashing out is still a disposal

Collecting rand cash at an ATM is not tax-free by default. Converting crypto to rand is a disposal for tax purposes, exactly as if you had sold it on an exchange. SARS treats crypto assets under normal tax principles: a gain can be revenue in nature (taxed as income, for frequent or short-term activity) or capital in nature (taxed under capital gains rules, for longer-term investment). The classification depends on the facts, not on which off-ramp you used.

Keep a record of the date, the rand value you received and the crypto’s cost basis, because you will need those numbers when you declare. South Africa’s Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) took effect on 1 March 2026, and relevant service providers must report certain user and transaction information to SARS — but individual users still declare their own gains in the normal return. See SARS’s crypto assets & tax page and our Crypto tax in South Africa guide for what to track.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a bank account or card to use CryptoExpress?

No. The withdrawal is cardless — you collect cash with your mobile number and a voucher PIN. You do need a crypto wallet from which to send the asset.

Is it safe, and is it regulated?

CryptoExpress is operated by Paycorp, a well-known South African payments company, and it applies KYC and AML screening with Triple-A handling the conversion and compliance. As with any crypto transfer, the moment you control is the send step — use the correct network and only ever deal inside the app. It is a cash-out tool, not a place to hold funds or trade.

Can I withdraw more than R5,000 at once?

Only by making multiple withdrawals, given the reported R5,000 per-withdrawal cap. For larger amounts, a licensed exchange bank withdrawal is the better path — see our Cash Out overview.

What if I send on the wrong network?

A wrong-network transfer may not be recoverable, so this is the one step to slow down on. Confirm the network in the app before you send, and contact CryptoExpress support (086 100 44 44 or support@cryptoexpress.co.za) immediately if something goes wrong — but prevention matters far more than recovery here.

Need more than R5,000 out? Use an exchange

For anything beyond a small cash withdrawal, a licensed exchange bank transfer is usually cheaper, faster and easier to document.
