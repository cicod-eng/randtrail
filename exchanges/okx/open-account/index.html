How to Open an OKX Account in South Africa

Last updated: 12 September 2026

Opening an OKX account in South Africa is free and takes a few minutes. You sign up with an email address and phone number, verify your identity using a government-issued ID document or passport, complete a short face check, and identity verification is usually completed quickly — though OKX does not publish a fixed review time and some applications need extra checks. Depositing rand is a separate step that comes after, and on OKX that route is built mainly around a P2P market.

The part that trips most people up is verification, not registration. Get your ID document right the first time and the whole thing is straightforward. For the full picture of OKX’s fees, ZAR access and regulatory status before you commit, see our OKX South Africa review.

Quick answer

  • Signing up is free — create an account with an email address and a phone number, then set a password. A referral code is optional.
  • Verification uses a government-issued ID — a passport, a South African ID (national identity card) or another supported government-issued document, plus a face check.
  • You must be 18 or older — identity verification is not available to minors.
  • Usually quick, no fixed timeframe — most reviews complete fast, but some need extra checks. Watch the verification page and your email.
  • ZAR after signup is mainly P2P — check your own account for other fiat options before you rely on a route.
  • Fund afterwards — depositing rand is a separate step after the account is open and verified.

What you need before you start

  • An email address you control and a phone number — OKX binds both to the account.
  • A South African ID document or passport — the original, not a copy or screenshot.
  • A few minutes in good lighting for the face check.
  • You must be at least 18 years old.

Step 1 — Create your account

Go to the official OKX website or download the official OKX app, then click Sign up. OKX’s registration help page lays out the flow:

  1. Select your country or region (South Africa) and accept the terms.
  2. Enter your email address — and a referral code if you have one.
  3. Enter the 6-digit code sent to that email (you usually have about 10 minutes).
  4. Enter and verify your phone number with its country code, then enter the 6-digit code sent to your phone.
  5. Create a password combining numbers, letters, symbols and mixed case.

Your account is created at this point, but most features stay locked until you verify your identity. Note that OKX allows one account per email and phone number, and one identity document can only be linked to one OKX account.

Referral note: If you register through a referral link, check that the referral code is shown as linked before you finish. OKX says referral relationships are confirmed only through referral codes, so a code added incorrectly cannot simply be fixed later.

Step 2 — Verify your identity (KYC)

South African law requires exchanges to verify a user’s identity before they can deposit or trade — this is the FICA-related “know your customer” step. On OKX, go to your profile and open Verification (or Identity verification), then follow the individual-account flow.

  1. Confirm your country of residence and nationality — it must match your ID documents.
  2. Enter your personal details exactly as they appear on your government-issued ID.
  3. Choose an accepted document. Per OKX’s identity verification help page, accepted documents include a passport, national identity card (your SA ID) and driver’s licence, among other government-issued documents — the exact list shown on your verification screen is the one that counts.

Proof of address is not part of the standard identity check, but OKX may request it for higher verification levels or higher limits. On the regulatory side, as of our latest check in September 2026, OKX does not appear on the FSCA’s published list of authorised crypto-asset service providers (CASPs) — it operates through its global entity. See our OKX review for the detail on what that means.

Step 3 — Face verification

Follow the on-screen instructions to complete face recognition — a live check that matches you to the document you uploaded. Make sure your face is clearly visible, with no hats or glasses blocking it and enough lighting. A failed facial check is one of the most common reasons a verification is rejected, so take your time on this step.

Step 4 — Turn on two-factor authentication

Before you fund the account, enable two-factor authentication (2FA) for logins, withdrawals and security changes. An authenticator app (OKX Authenticator or Google Authenticator) is generally preferable to relying on SMS alone, and a passkey can provide strong phishing resistance. This is the single highest-impact step for keeping the account safe — see account security for crypto for the full checklist.

How long verification takes — and what to do if it fails

OKX does not publish a fixed review time. Most verifications are completed quickly — often within minutes — but some require additional checks and take longer. Watch the verification page and your registered email, and avoid re-submitting while the status shows Submitted or Under review.

If it is rejected, you will be told what to fix. The most common reasons are a blurry or incomplete document photo, details that do not match the document, an expired or unsupported document, a failed facial check, or an identity document already linked to another account. Correct the issue and resubmit.
