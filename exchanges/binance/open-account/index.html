How to Open a Binance Account in South Africa

Last updated: 12 September 2026

Opening a Binance account in South Africa is free and takes a few minutes. You sign up with an email, verify your identity using a South African ID document or passport, complete a short face check, and identity verification is often completed within minutes — though it can take up to 24 hours. Depositing rand is a separate step that comes after.

The part that trips most people up is verification, not registration. Get your ID document right the first time and the whole thing is straightforward. For the full picture of Binance’s fees, ZAR routes and safety before you commit, see our Binance South Africa review.

Quick answer

  • Signing up is free — create an account with an email address, phone number, Apple account or Google account.
  • Verification uses one of three documents — your South African ID number (recommended), a South African National ID card, or a passport issued by the Department of Home Affairs.
  • No proof of address is required at the verification step.
  • Face check — no hats, glasses or filters, and enough lighting.
  • Usually instant, up to 24 hours — you get an email once your account is verified.
  • Fund afterwards — depositing ZAR is a separate step after the account is open.

What you need before you start

  • An email address you control (and a phone number for security).
  • A South African ID document or passport — the original, not a copy or screenshot.
  • A few minutes in good lighting for the face check.
  • Your current residential address details.

Step 1 — Create your account

Go to the official Binance website (binance.com/en-ZA) or download the official Binance app, then click Sign Up. Binance’s registration FAQ lays out the flow:

  1. Enter your email address or phone number — or use the Apple or Google sign-up options.
  2. Enter the 6-digit code (OTP) sent to that email to confirm the address.
  3. Create a password. Binance requires 8–128 characters with at least one number and one uppercase letter.

Your account is created at this point, but it is limited until you verify your identity.

Referral note: If you register through a referral link, check that the referral code is shown as linked before completing registration. Binance says a linked referral code cannot be changed afterwards.

Step 2 — Verify your identity (KYC)

South African law requires exchanges to verify a user’s identity before they can deposit or trade — this is the FICA-related “know your customer” step. On Binance, press Verify or go to User Center → Identification → Start Now. For the legal backdrop, see is Binance legal in South Africa.

  1. Select South Africa as your country of residence — it must match your ID documents.
  2. Enter your details exactly as they appear on your government-issued ID: nationality, first and last name, optional middle name, date of birth and residential address. Binance states that no proof of address is required at this step.
  3. Choose one of the accepted documents. Per Binance’s South African ID verification FAQ, these are your South African ID number (recommended), a South African National ID card, or a passport issued by the Department of Home Affairs.

Step 3 — Face verification

Follow the on-screen instructions to take a short selfie or video. Binance’s guidance is to not wear hats or glasses, not use filters, and make sure the lighting is sufficient. Turn off beauty filters or photo enhancements before you start, because Binance instructs users not to use filters during face verification. Use the original supported identity document and follow the capture instructions shown in the app.

Step 4 — Turn on two-factor authentication

Before you fund the account, enable two-factor authentication (2FA) for logins, withdrawals and security changes. An authenticator app (Binance Authenticator or Google Authenticator) is generally preferable to relying on SMS alone, and a passkey can provide strong phishing resistance. This is the single highest-impact step for keeping the account safe.

How long verification takes — and what to do if it fails

Verification is usually instant but can take up to 24 hours, after which you receive an email notification. If it is rejected, you will be told what to fix; correcting the issue and resubmitting is usually enough. The most common reasons for rejection are a poor-quality photo (glare, blur, or cut-off corners), a name that does not match your registration details, or an expired document.

After your account is open

An open and verified account is where the actual journey starts, not ends:

  • Deposit ZAR — fund the account with rand (Absa Pay, Capitec Pay, card, Apple Pay or bank transfer).
  • Buy USDT with ZAR — your first purchase once the rand lands.
  • Withdraw to your bank — how money comes back out when you need it.
