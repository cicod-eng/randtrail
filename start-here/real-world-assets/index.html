Real-World Assets (RWA) in South Africa (2026)

Last updated: 25 September 2026

A real-world asset (RWA) is a physical or financial asset whose value or ownership is represented on a blockchain. In South Africa, that can mean anything from a fraction of a physical Krugerrand to a token linked to Tesla shares or a private-credit portfolio.

South Africans can already access several kinds of tokenised real-world assets. The important part is understanding that not every RWA token gives you the same legal rights: some represent direct ownership of an underlying asset, while others provide only economic or price exposure.

Product availability, fees and eligibility can change quickly. Always check the current terms inside the platform before buying.

Quick verdict

  • South Africans can already access tokenised gold, U.S. stocks and ETFs, and private credit.
  • Binance relaunched tokenised stocks in 2026, but not for South Africans. Its bStocks product went live in June 2026, yet it is issued under an Abu Dhabi (ADGM) prospectus and restricted to eligible users in permitted jurisdictions — South Africa is not confirmed as eligible, so Binance does not currently offer tokenised stocks to South African users.
  • Luno and VALR also offer tokenised U.S. stocks to South African users. Luno starts from R20, while VALR offers xStocks through its local platform.
  • Mesh.trade offers tokenised Krugerrand ownership. Its TroyGold Coin (TGLD) has an advertised minimum of R50 for primary-market purchases, while its secondary market may allow smaller trades depending on available orders.
  • VALR also provides tokenised private-credit exposure through USDPC, currently with a minimum subscription of 5,000 USDC or equivalent through its OTC desk.
  • Do not assume a stock token means you own the share. Products such as VALR xStocks and Binance bStocks are backed by underlying securities but do not give the holder direct ownership of the listed company’s shares.

What are real-world assets (RWAs)?

Tokenisation turns rights linked to an existing asset — such as gold, shares, bonds or private loans — into blockchain-based tokens.

The underlying asset still matters most. A token backed by a Krugerrand ultimately depends on the gold behind it. A token linked to Tesla depends on the structure used to hold the corresponding shares. A private-credit token depends on borrowers repaying the underlying loans.

Blockchain technology can make these assets easier to divide, transfer and settle, but it does not automatically make them safer or give every token holder direct legal ownership.

That distinction matters because two products that both call themselves “tokenised assets” can work very differently.

The sector has expanded rapidly. Binance Research reported that active tokenised RWAs increased by about 589% from early 2025 to June 2026, while tokenised stocks were among the fastest-growing categories. Different market trackers use different definitions, however, so market-size estimates should be treated as indicative rather than exact.

The main RWA categories currently accessible to South African users are tokenised gold, tokenised equities and ETFs, and tokenised private credit.

Where Binance fits

For South African users, Binance’s RWA role is mainly tokenised gold and RWA tokens. Binance did relaunch tokenised equities in 2026, but that product is not available in South Africa — more on that below.

Tokenised gold

PAX Gold (PAXG) and Tether Gold (XAUt) provide blockchain-based exposure to physical gold. These are different from Mesh’s Krugerrand structure: you are buying a token issued by the relevant gold-token issuer rather than fractional ownership through Mesh’s locally issued TGLD product.

Tokenised stocks: bStocks — not available in South Africa

Binance did relaunch tokenised stocks in 2026. Its bStocks product went live on 11 June 2026 and quickly passed US$500 million in assets, with listings tied to companies such as Tesla, NVIDIA, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta and Palantir.

But bStocks is not a product South African users can currently access. It is issued by BTech Holdings Limited under an Approved Prospectus in the Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) only, and Binance states the offering is not made in any other jurisdiction. Access is limited to eligible users in permitted jurisdictions — the United States and the European Union are explicitly excluded, and South Africa is not on the confirmed eligible list.

So the accurate position for a South African reader is: Binance does not currently offer tokenised stocks to South African users. The global product exists, but it is not open to this market. If that changes, it will appear inside a verified Binance account rather than in a global announcement.

That also means a bStock is not a route to direct share ownership even where it is available — it is a tokenised security representing an interest in underlying securities, not direct registered ownership of the listed-company share.

For tokenised U.S. stocks that South Africans can buy, use Luno, VALR or Bitget instead. If you are new to Binance, see our Binance South Africa review and How to buy USDT with ZAR first.

The key difference is not simply which platform lists the most assets. It is what legal and economic rights the token gives you.

Luno says its tokenised stocks are backed by actual underlying shares held in regulated custody, but buying the token does not mean you directly own those shares.

VALR similarly describes xStocks as price-exposure tokens. Its documentation states that xStock holders do not receive direct ownership, voting rights or dividend rights in the underlying company.

Bitget’s Reality-issued rTokens are designed to track corresponding U.S. stocks or ETFs and generally do not provide shareholder voting rights. Bitget says cash dividends are handled separately and can be credited in USDT for supported products.

Mesh is structurally different: its TroyGold Coin represents fractional or full ownership of physical Krugerrands stored at Brink’s in Johannesburg.

Mesh.trade: tokenised Krugerrands from R50

For South African readers, Mesh’s TroyGold Coin is one of the clearest examples of an RWA because the connection between the token and the underlying physical asset is relatively easy to understand.

Each TGLD token represents one 1oz bullion Krugerrand, and the token can be fractionalised.

Mesh says the underlying coins are:

  • stored at Brink’s in Johannesburg;
  • fully allocated;
  • independently audited;
  • insured; and
  • represented by downloadable ownership certificates.

Mesh currently states a R50 minimum investment threshold for primary purchases. Once your holdings reach one full ounce, the token can generally be redeemed for a physical Krugerrand, subject to the applicable process and delivery costs.

The gold stored at Brink’s is also described by Mesh as insured through Lloyd’s of London.

This makes TGLD different from simply buying a gold-tracking derivative: the structure is intended to represent ownership of allocated physical bullion.

Luno tokenised stocks

Luno offers tokenised stocks and ETFs to verified customers in South Africa.

The published minimum is currently R20 per transaction, with a 1% trade cost listed in Luno’s South African fee schedule.

Available products include tokens linked to major companies such as Apple, Tesla and NVIDIA, as well as ETFs tracking broader markets such as the S&P 500.

But you are not directly buying the underlying U.S. share.

Luno explains that its stock tokens are backed by corresponding shares held in regulated custody. The investor owns the token rather than becoming the registered shareholder of the company represented by that token.

That distinction should be understood before comparing a tokenised-stock product with a conventional brokerage account.

VALR: xStocks and private credit

VALR gives South African users access to two different RWA categories.

xStocks

VALR lists xStocks representing companies such as Tesla, NVIDIA, Coinbase, Robinhood and Circle.

The tokens are issued by Backed Assets (JE) Limited and backed by corresponding underlying shares held through the issuer’s custody structure. VALR itself acts as the secondary trading venue rather than the issuer.

VALR currently limits its xStocks service to customers residing in South Africa and supports trading through its exchange and Simple Buy/Sell interface.

Again, holding an xStock is not direct ownership of the company’s share.

USD Private Credit Token

VALR also offers the USD Private Credit Token (USDPC).

USDPC provides exposure to a portfolio of senior secured, asset-backed private loans managed by Garrington Capital. The product targets an annual USD-denominated return of 8–10%, but that is a target rather than a guaranteed return.

Current published terms include:

  • minimum subscription: 5,000 USDC or equivalent;
  • purchases through VALR’s OTC desk;
  • purchase with USDC or USDT;
  • standard redemption liquidity of roughly 7–30 days;
  • same-day liquidity only on a best-efforts basis;
  • 0.10% subscription spread; and
  • 0.10% standard redemption spread.

Private credit introduces a different set of risks from tokenised stocks or gold, particularly borrower credit risk, liquidity risk and counterparty risk.

How to buy an RWA in South Africa

There is no single buying route because the process depends on the asset.

Option 1: Buy a tokenised stock locally

For a simpler ZAR-based route, verified South African Luno users can start from R20.

VALR also allows South African users to access xStocks, including through Simple Buy/Sell using supported assets such as ZAR, USDT, USDC or BTC.

Option 2: Buy tokenised physical gold

On Mesh.trade:

  1. create and verify an account;
  2. fund the account;
  3. find TroyGold Coin (TGLD);
  4. choose the amount of Krugerrand exposure you want; and
  5. complete the purchase.

You do not need enough money to purchase an entire one-ounce Krugerrand. TGLD can be fractionalised.

Option 3: Buy tokenised private credit

USDPC works differently. It is currently accessed through VALR’s OTC service and requires a substantially higher minimum of 5,000 USDC or equivalent.

What do you actually own?

This is the most important section of the guide.

Do not group all RWAs together simply because they use blockchain technology.

In other words, “1:1 backed” and “I own the share” are not interchangeable statements.

A platform may hold or arrange custody of one underlying share for each token while the token holder still has different legal rights from a shareholder whose name is held through a conventional brokerage structure.

This is why the issuer, prospectus, redemption terms, dividend treatment and voting rights matter as much as the token price.

Regulation in South Africa

South Africa formally declared crypto assets a financial product under the Financial Advisory and Intermediary Services Act (FAIS) in October 2022. This brought businesses providing specified financial services relating to crypto assets within the FSCA regulatory framework.

Crypto Asset Service Providers conducting regulated activities are required to obtain appropriate authorisation from the FSCA.

For example:

  • VALR (Pty) Ltd identifies itself as a licensed financial services provider, FSP #53308.
  • Mesh.trade received a CASP licence from the FSCA in July 2024 and says it holds Category I and II financial-services authorisation.

That does not mean every tokenised asset is regulated in exactly the same way.

A gold-ownership token, a crypto asset, a tokenised security and an interest in a private-credit partnership can have different legal structures. Global platforms may also distribute particular products under offshore regulatory frameworks.

For that reason, check both:

  1. the status of the platform, and
  2. the legal structure of the specific token.

An FSCA licence should not be interpreted as a guarantee of returns or a government endorsement of an investment.

Main risks

Tokenisation removes some barriers but introduces others.

  • Issuer risk: you depend on the entity that creates and maintains the token.
  • Custody risk: a token can be fully backed while the underlying asset is still held by a third-party custodian.
  • Counterparty risk: private-credit and similar products depend on borrowers and other counterparties meeting their obligations.
  • Liquidity risk: a blockchain token is not automatically easy to sell. Thin markets can produce large spreads or delays.
  • Tracking risk: the token price can temporarily differ from the value of the underlying asset.
  • Shareholder-rights risk: many stock tokens do not give you voting or other rights associated with directly owning shares.
  • Platform risk: access, withdrawals and available markets can change depending on the provider or jurisdiction.
  • Regulatory risk: tokenised securities are still developing across multiple legal frameworks, and product availability can change.

For beginners, checking the issuer, backing, custody arrangement and redemption rights is more useful than simply asking whether an RWA token is “backed”.

Tax: keep records from day one

Tokenisation does not make gains tax-free.

SARS states that normal income-tax rules apply to crypto assets and that taxpayers must declare relevant gains or losses. Depending on the facts and the taxpayer’s activity, gains may be treated as revenue or capital in nature.

South Africa’s Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework also took effect in March 2026, increasing reporting obligations for relevant crypto-asset service providers.

Keep records of:

  • purchase date;
  • ZAR value at purchase;
  • fees;
  • sales or redemptions;
  • dividends or distributions;
  • transfers between platforms; and
  • supporting transaction statements.

Frequently asked questions

Can South Africans buy tokenised real-world assets?

Yes. South African users can already access products including tokenised gold, tokenised U.S. equities and ETFs, and tokenised private credit.

The regulatory structure depends on both the provider and the individual product, so check the platform’s current FSCA status and the terms governing the token before buying.

Does Binance offer tokenised stocks in 2026?

Not to South African users.

Binance did relaunch tokenised stocks — its bStocks product went live on 11 June 2026 and kept expanding during the months that followed. It is a real, live product.

But bStocks is issued under an Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) prospectus and restricted to eligible users in permitted jurisdictions, with the United States and the European Union excluded. South Africa is not on the confirmed eligible list, so Binance does not currently make tokenised stocks available to South Africans.

This is different from the stock tokens Binance discontinued in 2021 — the 2026 product is a new tokenised-securities structure issued by BTech Holdings Limited. The key point for South African users is that they cannot access it right now.

What is the cheapest way to start?

Among the products covered in this guide:

  • Luno currently lists a R20 minimum for tokenised-stock purchases.
  • Mesh advertises a R50 minimum for TGLD primary-market investment, while its secondary-market rules can differ.

Minimum size should not be the only deciding factor. Fees, spreads, legal rights and withdrawal or redemption rules can matter more than whether an app lets you start with R20 or R50.

Are tokenised stocks the same as owning shares?

Usually not.

Luno and VALR both make clear that their tokenised-stock products do not mean you directly own the underlying company share. Binance similarly states that bStocks represent an interest in underlying securities but do not give the holder direct ownership of the underlying listed-company shares.

Always read the specific product terms rather than assuming that all tokenised-stock structures work the same way.

Can I redeem a tokenised stock for the real share?

It depends on the product.

VALR states that xStocks held through VALR cannot be redeemed for the underlying stock on its platform.

Redemption rights vary by product and issuer, so always read the specific product terms rather than treating all stock tokens as interchangeable.

Can I redeem tokenised gold for a physical Krugerrand?

With Mesh’s TroyGold Coin, yes — once your holdings reach the equivalent of one full ounce, Mesh says you can request redemption and arrange physical delivery of the corresponding Krugerrand, subject to applicable procedures and costs.

Is USDPC a guaranteed 8–10% investment?

No.

VALR describes 8–10% as the product’s target annual USD return, not a guarantee. USDPC exposes investors to a portfolio of private loans and therefore carries credit, liquidity, counterparty and capital-loss risk.

Bottom line

RWA investing in South Africa is no longer just a future concept.

South Africans can already access:

  • fractional physical Krugerrands through Mesh;
  • tokenised U.S. stocks and ETFs through Luno and VALR;
  • stock-linked rTokens through Bitget where available; and
  • tokenised private credit through VALR.

But the label “RWA” does not tell you what you legally own.

Before buying any tokenised asset, check four things:

What backs the token? Who holds that asset? What rights does the token give you? How can you redeem or sell it?

Those four questions tell you far more than the word “tokenised” on its own.

Start with Binance

For tokenised gold (PAXG, XAUt) and the broader crypto leg, Binance is the most direct route for most South Africans. For tokenised U.S. stocks — which Binance does not currently offer to South African users — use Luno, VALR or Bitget.
